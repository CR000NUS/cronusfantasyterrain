STANDING DECISIONS (in force)

1. Border terrain is frozen (2026-10-10)
The user said: "The terrain of the border is fine now. Do NOT change it from now on until i specifically say otherwise. If you have to change it for a reason, then first tell me so before actually changing it."
This covers the border mountain shape: RegionLayout terrain, border width, region partition and terrain density outputs. Material-only changes need their own explicit authorization.
Revised on request 2026-10-15 (decisions 6 and 7); the freeze applies to the revised shape.
Renewed and widened 2026-10-16 (after update_03_borderfin). The user said: "Now that the border of the regions look good, from now on do NOT change any of those unless i tell you to change it. If you have to change it in order to make biomes inside the region work, tell me."
The freeze now covers everything about the borders: mountain shape, region partition and rim lobes (decisions 8-12), border width, terrain density outputs, border materials and snow, the deepslate basin, and the border biome. Work on biomes inside the regions must not touch any of it; if that work seems to need a border change, tell the user and wait for their decision.

2. Border materials (2026-10-14 drawing, updated 2026-10-15)
- Exposed faces: deepslate (plain, no tuff) below the snow.
- Snow: natural, patchy transition around an average snowline of Y=214 (changed 2026-10-15, decision 7; band extended downward 2026-10-16: first patches from Y=179, full cap still from Y=264); no hard cut.
- No exterior stone, blackstone, tuff, ore or random patches.
- Underground: deepslate, about 4% tuff patches and deepslate ores.
- Digging into the mountains or their caves must reveal only deepslate, never stone.

5. Region basin / "cauldron" (2026-10-15, user sketch)
The user said: "Think of it like a of the regions like cauldrons. The deepslate (from underneath the stone layers of the overworld) all the way up the border mountains are the cauldron itself. The biome, the dirt floor, the stone underneath, all of that is the water inside the cauldron."
- Region interiors and foothills keep grass, dirt and stone when dug into.
- Below them a deepslate bowl rises from the vanilla deepslate line (Y=0) to the mountain foot and continues as the border mountains.

3. Border features
Only custom deepslate ore placements. No dirt, gravel, granite, diorite, andesite, vegetation or other decorative features.

4. Scorched Lands naming (2026-10-12)
Ember Lands was renamed Scorched Lands in documentation only. The code/data key stays ember_lands.

6. Higher border massif with a harsh transition (2026-10-15)
The user said: "Change the terrain of the border a bit more so that it's overall more leaning towards a higher altitude. There shouldn't be holes, lakes or cliffs at ground level or below anymore. The transitions from the region to the border should also be less slight and more harsh. It should still be a transition but a more harsh and quick transition."
- Massif floor Y=125, crests about Y=300; no belt terrain below region ground (Y=75).
- Ramp from region ground onto the massif within about 100 blocks; steep, but no sheer walls at ground level.
- Ravine carver removed from the border biome. Border width and region partition unchanged.

7. Broad mountain range and natural snowline (2026-10-15)
The user said the snow "starts with a hard cut, starting at a set world height. Change this so that snow has a natural looking transition."
And: "The border should look like 1 giant mountain range with many moutain peaks and rarely a deep cut. To put it into perspective, the majority of the border should be at a height between 70-100 blocks. A small amount has a chance to generate a mountain that reaches a height of 120 and a small amount has a chance to generate a valley that reaches a height of 60. It should look natural and realistic."
- The 60/70/100/120 figures were read as proportions of the range, with 120 at the previous highest crests (about Y=300): main band Y=175-250, rare summits to about Y=300, rare valleys to about Y=150.
- Smooth noise replaces the ridged multifractal, so mountains between valleys are broad, not thin and pointy.
- Ramp, border width and region partition unchanged.

8. Round regions with jagged rims (2026-10-15)
The user showed a sharp crease where two ramp walls met at a region corner and a long, uniform straight wall, and said: "So the regions should be more circular and the edges should be slightly more jagged. Since it's now a bit more circular shape, i want the radius of the region to be roughly somewhere between 1300-1500 blocks"
Sketches: one round region with a jagged rim; several irregular round regions with mountain gaps between them.
- Regions are round blobs, radius 1300-1500 blocks from centre to mountain foot, rim wobble up to about 60 blocks.
- Centres on a jittered hexagonal grid 3,800 blocks apart; mountains fill the gaps. Ownership still changes inside full mountains.
- Mountain shape, ramp, materials and snow unchanged.

9. Bigger regions, thinner borders, slightly sharper mountains (2026-10-15)
The user said: "Change the spacing a bit by making the regions slightly bigger and the borders a bit thinner." and "The mountains are a bit too smooth. Make them slightly more pointy/jagged again but not as much as it originally used to be."
- Region radius 1450-1650 blocks (was 1300-1500); spacing stays 3,800. Narrowest belt about 400 blocks foot to foot (was about 560). Ownership now changes halfway between two mountain feet.
- Moderate ridged crests added on top of the rounded peaks (RIDGE_STRENGTH = 55). Height bands unchanged: about 80% between Y=175 and Y=250.

10. Slightly less circular regions (2026-10-15)
The user said: "Make the regions slightly less circular."
- Per-region three-lobed shape (up to 8% of the radius, lobes pointing into the wide three-region mountain areas) and slight oval stretch (up to 6%), random strength and orientation.
- Radii are capped per direction, so the narrowest belt stays about 400 blocks. Median radius about 1495.

11. Rounded-hexagon regions, narrower borders (2026-10-15)
The user said: "Make the regions less circular so that the borders are not as wide anymore."
- Regions are rounded hexagons (HEX = 12) filling their share of the grid; the rim stays 200 blocks behind the halfway line to each neighbour in every direction. Belts about 400 blocks foot to foot everywhere, no wide three-region mountain areas.
- Per-region lobes and oval stretch reduced to 2% each (they cost space). Regions cover about 60% of the world (was about 50%).

12. Mountain foot reaching into the regions (2026-10-16)
The user asked for the rim of the border to "reach more randomly into the region itself" and drew a winding foot line with rounded tongues and bays.
- First attempt (low deepslate aprons in front of the ramp) rejected the same day: "too random", the gentle aprons did not blend in and ended at a sudden steep wall.
- Final: the whole mountain front (ramp and massif) pushes up to RIM_REACH = 180 blocks into a region in broad, smooth lobes and bays a few hundred blocks across, everywhere along the rim. No aprons; the ramp keeps its normal steep profile. Reach is one-sided, so no belt gets thinner, and it never forms detached islands.
- Region interiors (no border influence at all) shrink from about 60% to about 53% of the world.

13. Everwinter, first custom interior biome (2026-10-16)
The user asked to start on the regions themselves, first the Frozen Wastes, first biome Everwinter: "filled with winter, cold, ice and snow. The majority of the ground is covered with snow and ice blocks. Occasionally there are also parts of deepslate reaching out of the ground. The underground is a mix between deepslate and stone (and of course ores and stuff)." Terrain reference: concept image of snow plains, glacier shelves with fractured ice walls, ice shards and dark rock.
- Implemented as cronusfantasyterrain:everwinter in Frozen Wastes pool slot 2 of 5 (placeholders kept around it). See docs/biomes/everwinter.txt.
- Biome terrain is a new layer on top of the region terrain and fades to nothing before any border column; borders, basin and border materials are unchanged (decision 1 respected).

14. One district per biome in every region (2026-10-16)
The user said the biomes inside a region were "pretty mixed up like sometimes a biome appears twice and they appear rather small" and asked that each region has only 4-6 biomes and each appears only once in its region.
- Pools accept 4-6 biomes; a pool listing the same biome twice is rejected.
- Local biome noise replaced by BiomeDistricts: each region is split into one large, connected slice per pool biome (seeded ring Voronoi with gently warped boundaries, biome order shuffled per region). Slices cross the mountain foot steeply, so no slivers form along the rim.
- Everwinter relief now fades over 40 blocks from its district boundary. Borders, region partition and terrain are unchanged (decision 1 respected); RegionLayout.Sample only exposes the owning region cell and its local position.

15. Rougher Everwinter (2026-10-16)
The user asked for "a bit more elevation change", "hills made up of snow and ice (Small hills/piles made up of snow which appear more often and big snow/ice hills which appear a bit more rare)", "A few cliffs and tears in the ground which reveal deep ice crevasse", and "the terrain overall more harsh and rough to pass through", with two reference images (broken glacier with ice walls and a crevasse canyon; a jumbled field of ice blocks).
- Everwinter ground lifted about 10 blocks with larger swells; frequent small snow piles; rarer big snow/ice hills (icy ones with a sheer ice face); a few ice cliffs; long, deep, roughly parallel crevasses; glacier shelves replaced by serac fields of jumbled ice blocks split by cracks.
- A glacier ice sheet now lies under the snow cover and reaches below every nearby cut, so crevasse and cliff walls are ice. Cuts stop at Y=66 to stay dry.
- Relief fades in over 96 blocks (was 40) from the district boundary because the biome now sits higher than its neighbours. Borders untouched (decision 1).

16. Connected Everwinter terrain, snow layers, varied seracs (2026-10-16)
After testing decision 15 in-game the user found the ground "very flat and boring" with the features "just put on top of it", and asked for snow layer transitions: "Do not make smooth transitions everywhere. Some parts should have smooth transitions, some should have a more harsh transition." "Only use the snow layer blocks when you have to. If you have to stack 5 snow layers, simply Use snow blocks instead. Only for the smooth transition use snow layers on top of snow blocks." The serac fields should be more random: bigger and smaller pieces, more spread out or packed, different heights, tall pillars and giant plates tilted 20-30 degrees, more broken ground.
- Ground: medium rolls and variable wind ridges; activity field clusters piles and hills; drift fields of merging piles; hills on broad feet with snow tails; serac fields on raised glacier ground; snow aprons around outcrops and against cliffs where sheltered.
- Shelter field: sheltered stretches get smooth transitions (long ramps, banked snow, snow layers), wind-scoured stretches harsh ones (short rises, sheer edges, block steps).
- Snow layers: generated by the material rule as the top block (1-4 layers on a snow block; 5+ would be a full block), only on sheltered snow ground with slopes of at most one block per block. freeze_top_layer removed from Everwinter because it resets layers to one.
- Serac pieces in four styles (tilted giant plates, blocks, close-packed pillars, rubble), varied heights, tilts of 20-30 degrees, packed or spread apart, debris beyond the margins. Borders untouched (decision 1).

17. Snow layers reverted, scattered serac plates (2026-10-17)
After the in-game test of decision 16 the user asked to revert the snow layer transitions and the removal of freeze_top_layer (stepped layer patterns; a hard, chunk-shaped edge against the neighbouring snowy biomes), keeping the shaped transitions between snow piles, hills and the rest. Serac fields were "way too many" and clustered: "Never ever have this many all cluster into one spot." No thin, tall, square pillars; instead "more randomly formed plates which create some more plateaus", tilted anywhere from 5 to 30 degrees.
- Snow layer blocks removed entirely (no snow_layers output); Everwinter has freeze_top_layer again. Shelter field kept for shaped smooth/harsh transitions.
- Serac fields placed on a jittered 450-block grid (at most one per square, half the squares), 45-100 blocks in radius; about 2-3% of the biome instead of 13%.
- Pieces are irregular plates (large about 30 blocks, about half split into about 15), each a plateau tilted 5-30 degrees; no pillars.

18. Separate serac plates (2026-10-17)
After the in-game test of decision 17 the user liked the hills and elevation changes but found the serac plates still packed into a cluttered mass with thin cracks: "SPREAD OUT EACH COLUMN! I DO NOT WANT THIS CLUTTERED MESS!" Sketches showed a few separate, irregular plates with wide gaps, and a side view of a tilted slab emerging from the ground at its low end with a sheer face (and a crack at its foot) at the high end.
- Each field holds a handful of separate plates (one per 52-block square at most), 10-22 blocks of open snow ground between neighbours; no splitting into smaller plates, no debris, no raised or lumpy field ground, no piles or outcrops in and around the field.
- Each plate is a tilted slab (5-30 degrees): ramp out of the ground at the low end, sheer ice face at the high end, narrow crack at the foot of tall faces. Serac fields now cover under 1% of the biome.

19. Serac plates are the ground, three times larger (2026-10-17)
The user: "Change it so that the glacier plates are becoming the new ground. It should not look like they are simply growing out of the ground but they ARE the ground which is tilted and rotated and split up." Sketch: the ground breaks off in a wall down to the plate's sunken low end; the plate rises to a sheer high face. Plates should be about 300% of their size, each between 250% and 350%.
- Each plate is a broken-off piece of the ground (keeping its rolls), tilted 5-30 degrees about its middle: low end sunk below the ground (at most 20 blocks), high end a sheer ice face with a crack at its foot; very long steep plates level off towards 45 blocks.
- Plate radius scaled by 2.5-3.5 per plate (about 95-195 blocks across); plate cells 156 blocks, gaps 16-36 blocks; fields 90-170 blocks in radius, at most one per 700-block square. Serac fields about 3% of the biome.

20. Serac plate groups, two or three per biome (2026-10-17)
After the in-game test of decision 19 the user liked the terrain and the tilted ground plates. "Whenever there is 1 broken glacier plate, there should be at least 3 and at most 6 or 7 others right next to it. Also scale them up a little bit more. Also give them a slightly higher chance to appear more often. Maybe those glacier-plate-groups of 3 to 7 of these plates can appear twice or thrice in the biome."
- Every serac field is a group of 4-7 plates (one in the middle, the rest in a ring), 16-36 blocks of snow ground between neighbours (the spacing of decision 18 kept).
- Plates scaled by another 1.2 (about 115-235 blocks across).
- Fields are placed per Everwinter district instead of on a random grid: two or three per district (a small district may get one), well inside the biome and apart from each other. Plates cover about 10-15% of the biome.

PROJECT STATUS

- Eleven regions confirmed. Five are generated with vanilla placeholder pools: Frozen Wastes, Cold Meadows, Scorched Lands, Verdant Valley, Crystal Peaks. Six are planned with no design yet: Blooming Highlands, Dry Dunes, Enchanted Groves, Land of the Giants, Misty Marshes, Shadow Wilds.
- Twenty vanilla placeholder biomes (four per region) plus two custom biomes: Region Border and Everwinter (Frozen Wastes).
- Frostscar is a planned custom biome for Frozen Wastes. Ice Desert is an earlier Test World prototype, not a biome.
- Biome terrain exists for Everwinter only. Not implemented: custom cave biomes, mountain passes, oceans or coastlines. The Nether and End are vanilla.
- Targets Minecraft 26.3, NeoForge 26.3.0.43-beta, Java 25.
