Everwinter
Recorded: 2026-10-16

Biome ID: cronusfantasyterrain:everwinter
Region: Frozen Wastes (pool slot 2 of 5; one district per region)
Status: Implemented custom biome (seventh version: two or three groups of 4-7 huge tilted ground plates per biome, 2026-10-17; awaiting in-game review).

User design (2026-10-16)
"It is a biome which is filled with winter, cold, ice and snow. The majority of the ground is covered with snow and ice blocks. Occasionally there are also parts of deepslate reaching out of the ground. The underground is a mix between deepslate and stone (and of course ores and stuff)."
Terrain reference: a concept image (snow plains with wind-shaped drifts, raised glacier shelves with steep fractured ice walls, ice shards, dark rock breaking through the snow, distant mountains).

User update (2026-10-16)
"Bring in a bit more elevation change. Create hills made up of snow and ice (Small hills/piles made up of snow which appear more often and big snow/ice hills which appear a bit more rare). A few cliffs and tears in the ground which reveal deep ice crevasse." "Make the terrain overall more harsh and rough to pass through."
References: the same concept image (snow foreground, a broken glacier shelf with columnar ice walls and a crevasse canyon, ice shards) and a close-up of a jumbled field of tabular ice blocks (seracs) separated by deep cracks.

Terrain (EverwinterTerrain, on top of the shared region interior around Y=80; shares measured by everwinterTest)
The features grow out of the ground instead of sitting on a flat plain, and a slowly varying shelter field (about half the biome each) decides how they meet it: sheltered stretches get long snow ramps and snow banked against cliffs and outcrops; wind-scoured stretches keep short steep rises and sheer edges. The transitions are shaped by the terrain itself (full blocks only; no generated snow layer blocks).
- Rolling ground: broad rises and hollows lifted about 10 blocks above the plain interior, medium rolls (about +/-4 blocks over 150 blocks) and wind ridges that are strong in some stretches and calm in others. 90% of the biome lies between about Y=85 and Y=115; extremes Y=66-164.
- Busy and calm ground: a large-scale activity field makes snow piles and big hills cluster in busy stretches, with calmer snow fields between.
- Snow piles (common, about 16% of the biome): wind-stretched heaps 2-9 blocks high and 6-22 blocks across (mostly small), steeper on the lee side. In busy ground they form dense drift fields where neighbouring piles merge into ridges. Pure snow.
- Big snow/ice hills (rarer, about 5% of the biome): mounds 18-42 blocks high, 45-85 blocks across, on a broad low foot (about twice as wide, snow ground) that merges into the surrounding ground, with a long snow tail stretching downwind. Snow hills are smooth; icy hills (about half) have a sheer wind-cut face of bare ice on one side and a craggy top.
- Ice cliffs (a few): the ground steps up 10-20 blocks along long winding lines. In wind-scoured stretches the step is mostly a sheer, ragged ice cliff; where sheltered it is mostly a slope, and the remaining cliffs have snow banked against their lower half.
- Crevasses (a few): long, roughly parallel, jagged tears in the ground (like crevasses across a flowing glacier), 3-12 blocks wide and about 18-34 blocks deep, tapering out at both ends; snow lip on top, ice walls down to the floor.
- Serac fields (two or three per biome, plates about 10-15% of the biome): groups of 4-7 huge pieces of the glacier ground itself, broken off and tilted, lying next to each other: one plate in the middle and the rest in a ring around it, with 16-36 blocks of unbroken snow ground between neighbouring plates (no piles, hills or outcrops on or within about 40 blocks of a plate). Each Everwinter district gets two or three fields (random per region; a small district may get only one), placed well inside the biome (full relief at the field's centre) and at least 50 blocks apart beyond their reach, so fields never merge into one. Plates have irregular, wobbly outlines about 115-235 blocks across (the decision-19 size scaled by another 1.2; cut back where they meet a neighbour) and keep the ground's own rolls on top. Each is tilted 5-30 degrees in its own direction about its middle: the low end sinks below the surrounding ground, which breaks off in an ice wall down to it (at most 20 blocks deep), and the high end rises to a sheer ice face (up to about 35-45 blocks above the ground on long, steep plates, which level off) with a narrow crack 6-14 blocks deep at its foot.
- Crevasses and cracks together cover well under 1% of the biome; floors never go below Y=66, so they stay dry (sea level 63).
- Ice spires: rare sharp, slightly leaning shards 8-28 blocks tall.
- Deepslate outcrops (about 3%): rough rock knobs up to 9 blocks above the snow, kept off serac fields and big hills; snow banks up around them where sheltered.
- The relief fades in over 96 blocks inside the biome edge and over 120 blocks from where the border mask reaches zero, so neighbouring biomes, the border mountains and the deepslate basin are untouched.

Materials (EverwinterPalette, Y >= 0; below Y=0 stays vanilla)
- Open ground: snow blocks 4 deep (1 on steep slopes), with patches of packed ice (about 12% of the surface).
- Glacier ice sheet beneath the snow: packed ice with thin tilted blue-ice layers, about 1-9 blocks thick on open ground, and reaching below the floor of every nearby crevasse or crack (and under and around serac fields), so cliffs, cracks and crevasse walls show ice, not rock.
- Snow piles, hill tails and banked snow: snow throughout (16 blocks deep). Big hills: 3 blocks of snow over ice, bare ice on steep faces.
- Serac plates and crevasse floors: glacier ice, with a single snow block on gentle tops (bare ice on steep ones). Spires: packed and blue ice.
- Outcrops: deepslate, with a little snow on flat tops.
- Underground: stone and deepslate in large blobs (about 46% deepslate between Y=0 and Y=70), deepslate more common deeper down. Vanilla ores and tuff (stone and deepslate ore variants), copper and iron veins, caves.
- Packed and blue ice are used instead of plain ice so torches do not melt the landscape.
- On top, vanilla freeze_top_layer adds a single snow layer as in the neighbouring snowy biomes.

Biome settings (biome/everwinter.json)
- Based on Snowy Plains: temperature -0.7, downfall 0.9, snowfall, frozen water, a single snow layer on top (freeze_top_layer, so the edges blend with the neighbouring snowy biomes).
- Mobs as Snowy Plains (rabbits, polar bears, strays among the monsters).
- Frozen Peaks music, slightly icier sky colour.
- No trees, flowers, grass, mushrooms, sugar cane or pumpkins; no dirt, gravel, granite, diorite, andesite, sand/clay/gravel disks or surface lava lakes.
